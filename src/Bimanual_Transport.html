Placement errors

Release requires horizontal error <4 cm, height error <1 cm, tilt <0.08 rad, speed <0.08 m/s and actual delivery-support contact.

Native arm torques

Full articulated inertia, gravity, load feedforward, bounded actuation, contacts and closed-chain constraints are integrated.

Grasp-position and contact residuals

Ideal fixed grasps and compliant contacts have numerical residuals. These values are reported without calling the motion perfectly rigid or collision-free.

Actual tray speed and tilt

Speed is in m/s; tilt is in rad. Final success additionally requires speed <0.03 m/s, valid joint ranges and bounded penetration.

How this connects to K-th task allocation

A dual-arm carry occupies the execution resources of one complete robot. It is not k=2 robot coverage: k counts independent physical robots. Inspection jobs can require two independent views, while a transport job reserves both arms of one agent. The allocation lab models a declared busy-resource interval; this page supplies a physically simulated transport cycle and its duration.

The present model uses setup-only position IK, fixed fingers and ideal fixed-grasp fixtures. It does not claim autonomous grasp acquisition, frictional finger grasping, slip robustness or real-robot control. The object itself has a free joint; its weight and constraint/contact forces are real simulation states.

Try left-only and no-grasp counterchecks. Their failed placement/release remains visible. Failure under this controller does not prove that a properly designed single-arm grasp could never carry the payload.
