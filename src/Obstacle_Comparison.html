Obstacles, safety, failure, and communication

Custom obstacles are a JSON array of disks, for example [{"x":0.1,"y":0.2,"r":0.15}]. Up to 20 disks; each must lie inside radius 0.90. Overlaps are treated as a union. The common outer boundary is the unit disk. Coordinates and radii use domain units.

Single-integrator plants: p_dot = u. Both use u_nom = gain (centroid - p), the same half-plane velocity projection, and exact straight-segment safety checks. Higher-order robot models remain available in the original simulator; this matched safety proof is specific to single integrators.

Assigned coverage cost H

H = (1 / 2k) integral of the assigned squared-distance sum, with total demand mass 1. Moving sector boundaries or failure events may increase H.

Same geometric score at both configurations

Uses the k nearest robots at both configurations, independently of the controller's assignment. Voronoi is optimal among size-k assignments at fixed positions; this does not establish which trajectory finds a better constrained configuration.

Responsibility-load imbalance

Same definition for both methods: RMS(load - k/N) / (k/N). Standard Voronoi descent does not impose load equality.

Applied robot velocity

Includes every safety and step-scale correction. The exported data also include peak velocity, input integral, and path length.

Minimum physical clearance

Distance between robot surfaces and obstacles, wall, or other robots. The exact swept minimum is exported as well; endpoints alone are insufficient for collision validation.

What changes algebraically?

Interpret the comparison before claiming a winner

The sector method explicitly balances overlapping loads, and complementary boundary orders can remove its arithmetic blind modes. Voronoi changes its responsibility hypergraph with positions and minimizes the Euclidean assigned-distance cost at each fixed configuration. The two mechanisms optimize different aspects of coverage. Compare cost, load, communication, and clearance together.

All computation uses a known shared map and reliable complete routed snapshots. Both methods deliberately use the same all-gather packet protocol, rather than claiming an optimized local Voronoi implementation. A disconnected carrier or an exhausted snapshot budget blocks that method's next update. The logical ring is an ideal carrier and ignores physical obstacle occlusion. A failed robot is removed, not left as an obstacle.

Nonempty cyclic sectors follow under the positive angular marginal assumption. This is a statement about demand sets, not about their connectivity. The safety filter can cause constrained stationary traps: safe motion does not imply centroid convergence or global optimality. The one-step robot-motion cost is checked separately from boundary and failure jumps.
